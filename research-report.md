# Research Report: UPI and Digital Payment Safety for Senior Citizens

## 1. Project question
How can AI help create simple, large-print safety material that protects senior citizens from digital payment scams?

## 2. Target audience
- Primary: senior citizens who use, or are learning to use, UPI and mobile banking.
- Secondary: family caregivers who support older adults without taking away their independence.
- Design needs: large print, high contrast, short sentences, familiar examples, no jargon, bilingual instructions and a clear emergency reminder.

## 4. Common scam patterns to explain
1. **OTP/PIN requests:** caller asks the target to reveal a secret code or enter it into an unfamiliar page.
2. **KYC/account-block threats:** urgent message says the account will be blocked unless the person follows a link.
3. **Fake customer-care support:** scammer impersonates support and asks for confidential details or remote access.
4. **QR/refund trick:** person is told to scan a QR code or enter a UPI PIN to receive money. A PIN entry may authorize a payment; do not assume a request is safe.
5. **Impersonation and urgency:** caller claims to be a relative, official or bank employee and demands immediate transfer.
6. **Prize/benefit fee:** unexpected prize or benefit requires an upfront payment or personal details.
7. **Malicious links/apps:** link or app may steal credentials or expose the device.
8. **Payment screen confusion:** recipient name and amount are not checked before authorizing a transaction.

These are educational categories, not a complete catalogue. Fraud tactics change.

## 6. How AI helps
AI can simplify technical language, generate fictional scenarios, create quiz distractors, translate drafts and suggest accessible layouts. AI can also invent citations, misstate helpline information, mistranslate security concepts or present outdated procedures. Therefore, AI output is a draft; official sources and human review are required.
